## The narrative

The deck is two chapters plus a call to action. In order:

**1.0 Why we exist**
1. **A fast-changing, fracturing world.** Change is not slowing, and the institutions that
   protected access to the basics are breaking down.
2. **The ingredients for thriving stay the same:** a decent home, nutritious food,
   accessible healthcare, quality education, safety.
3. **Access to thriving is declining.** Social mobility is falling across the Western world,
   which the notes attribute to failing to adapt with new tools.
4. **Proven grassroots models.** Neighbourhoods have shown they can identify problems and
   deliver solutions. Examples named: Fernwood (Victoria), Evergreen Cooperatives
   (Cleveland), Parkdale Neighbourhood Land Trust (Toronto), and a community-owned Toronto
   music venue.
5. **Infrastructure is forming to scale them.** Employee Ownership Canada, Tapestry
   Community Capital and the Canadian Coalition for Community Capital are cited. The
   headline figure: redirecting 1% of Canadian investment into local communities would
   unlock **$42B**.

**2.0 Building local capacity**
1. **Reducing risk.** Getting Canadians to invest $42B requires neighbourhoods to identify
   problems, design viable solutions and execute reliably.
2. **Living labs.** Borrow the agile cycle enterprises use to de-risk investment (insight,
   action, impact measurement, adaptation) and extend the living-labs model so
   neighbourhoods are stakeholders in solving their own problems.
3. **Digital infrastructure.** Imagine Cities' stated role: "the research, learning and
   measurement layer". It builds digital learning infrastructure, gives local leaders
   frameworks to identify problems and run projects, and standardizes tools so risk falls
   for residents and investors alike.
4. **Talent for the new economy.** Training neighbourhood leaders also builds the
   middle-management talent the innovation economy lacks: curiosity, growth mindset,
   empathy, collaborative problem solving, adaptation.
5. **Support us.** Follow on social media, refer someone who could use the services,
   volunteer. Contact: connect@imaginecities.com.

The speaking notes close on a partner pitch: **"Build the Future of Neighbourhoods With
Us"**, asking organizations to partner to de-risk neighbourhood investment and scale
solutions nationwide.

## The first concrete product: Adaptive Leadership Certificate

The form is effectively a finished course page, and the first concrete version of
"educational courses" (product 1 in `strategy-2026-03.md`).

- **Pitch:** the same $42B and de-risking argument, applied to training. More neighbourhoods
  able to find problems, deliver solutions and measure results means less perceived risk.
- **Learning model, five principles:** phenomenon-based learning; authentic project-based
  assessment on the participant's own neighbourhood; Socratic teaching and autonomy
  (facilitation, not lectures); collaborative problem solving; embracing struggle.
- **Who it's for:** leaders in neighbourhood organizations first, open to government,
  business and nonprofit people.
- **Format:** cohort-based. A start date is proposed once 10 people register.
- **Instructor:** Ron Jaicarran, founder of Remarkable Future, named on the public form.
- **Also offered:** a quarterly newsletter sign-up.

## What changed since March 2026

- **A new audience: investors and community-capital partners.** March listed government,
  foundations and community practitioners. The new materials argue to people deciding
  where capital goes, and to partner organizations building that ecosystem.
- **"Leaders", not "residents".** Both the About line and the certificate address leaders.
- **Measurement is now the centre of the story.** The assessment tool
  (`observed-assessment-tool.md`) fits directly: it is the "measurement" in research,
  learning and measurement.
- **Not mentioned:** the LEARN · SOLVE · GROW framework, hackathons, and membership. Whether
  they are dropped or simply not part of this pitch is not stated.

## Implications for website copy

Copy consequences, not brand changes:

- **The deck is a ready-made site structure.** Home carries the hero line and a short
  version of the story. "Why we exist" and "What we do" (Building local capacity) become
  pages. "Support us" becomes Get Involved. The certificate becomes the first program page.
- **Two registers, kept apart.** The hero line is warm and works for every reader. The
  $42B, de-risking, agile and new-economy language is investor-facing and reads as the
  consultancy-report register `PRODUCT.md` warns against. Keep it on a partners or funders
  page, not on pages for community leaders and volunteers.
- **Every claim will be read by a procurement officer or funder.** Statistics need sources
  on the page.
