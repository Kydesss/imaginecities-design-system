## Positioning

The organization positions itself as a **capability builder**: teaching innovation
knowledge and skills to advance a culture of innovation, by building thriving
neighbourhoods. Its stated core value proposition is helping people in neighbourhoods
build skills *while* improving their neighbourhoods, at the same time.

The stated identity is building thriving neighbourhoods while developing local talent, and
the durability claim is that capacity is left behind — people embedded in networks and
systems that outlast any single engagement.

## Audiences

This is the biggest change for design. The organization now sells to institutions and
serves residents, so any interface has to speak to three distinct readers:

| Tier | Who | What they buy |
|---|---|---|
| **Primary** | Provincial government and municipalities | Assessments · workshops · training |
| **Secondary** | Foundations and non-profits | Assessments · impact reporting · training |
| **End user** | Community practitioners and community associations | Assessments · training · lab |

The end users are described as cash-poor but the actual unit of change, while the buyers
are institutions with procurement budgets who need measurable outcomes. A single page often
has to satisfy a procurement reader and a volunteer community leader at once.

## Products

Four core products, under a **LEARN · SOLVE · GROW** framework:

1. **Educational courses** — structured skill building, self-directed or cohort.
2. **Problem solving and hackathons** — time-bounded facilitation producing an
   implementation plan.
3. **Neighbourhood assessments and measurement** — diagnostic tools that baseline a
   neighbourhood, used by funders to validate grants and by municipalities to target
   programs.
4. **Membership and peer networks** — annual membership giving ongoing access to courses,
   data, and a peer network.

Revenue comes from training contracts, facilitation retainers, tiered memberships,
assessment fees, and sponsorship.

### This explains the assessment tool

The Neighbourhood Adaptability Assessment Flow audited in
`observed-assessment-tool.md` is product 3. It is not a side experiment — it is one of four
core products, and the one both institutional buyers use to justify spend. Its brand
compliance matters more than its prototype status suggests.

## Implications for the design system

Design consequences, not brand changes:

- **Three audiences, one system.** Institutional credibility and community warmth have to
  coexist. The palette supports this — Deep Azure and Charcoal for institutional surfaces,
  Gold and the tints for community-facing ones — without needing new colours.
- **Assessments and reporting need data display.** Diagnostics, baselines and impact
  reporting imply charts, scales and result states. v2.0.1 documents none of that, and the
  gap is now product-critical rather than theoretical.
- **Courses imply long-form reading.** Guides and cohort material need body copy, lists and
  hierarchy at length. The twelve documented type styles cover this, but the missing
  spacing scale is felt hardest here.
- **Membership implies authenticated states.** Accounts, dashboards and gated content — all
  undocumented component territory.

## A question for the brand owner

The v2.0.1 messaging and this strategy do not describe the same organization in the same
words. The brand deck's About text is about providing residents with resources to learn and
plan; the strategy leads with talent pipelines, the innovation economy, and selling to
municipalities and foundations.

Both can be true, but the brand's written voice predates the strategy by roughly eighteen
months. **Whether the About text and tagline should be revised to match is a brand-owner
decision.** It is flagged here, not acted on — this repo does not rewrite brand copy.
